## What the evidence supports

V8 is the strongest learned-replacement result: 9/9 fresh checkpoint-by-seed
cases reproduced for an independently fitted **affine output projection**.
The original nonlinear feature computation remained. This is the best method
for that target class, not evidence that it replaces whole nonlinear blocks.
V28 repeats the strong treatment result on fresh targets, but its frozen
mapping-permutation tolerance gate failed; the complete outcome remains FAIL.

The exact additive split is a mathematical positive control. Earlier symmetry
and mapped-intervention runs identify coordinate invariance. Neither is a
demonstration of independently learned whole-mechanism replacement.

## Decision for the next run

1. Replace **both entire nonlinear blocks**, with no residual bypass, rather
   than fitting the known linear projection class again.
2. Require guide task competence and measured target-removal harm using only
   visible qualification data. Stop as NO_QUALIFIED_GUIDE if this fails.
3. Train matched activation-interchange outcomes through the downstream model;
   include new combinations of interventions and fresh contexts at evaluation.
4. Fit the already-converted prefix at stage two. Distribution shift from
   earlier replacements is part of the mechanism under test.
5. Compare pointwise alignment, output distillation, and a clearly specified
   staged CKA plus final task-training implementation. No claim of reproducing
   the original published benchmark or winning it.
6. Separate absolute retention from comparative superiority, enforce identical
   update/data budgets, seal all predictions before oracle outcomes, and
   reproduce the complete frozen run in a distinct runtime.

This decision is a hypothesis, not assurance of success. No old failed receipt
is edited, threshold retuned, or unfavorable seed omitted. Novelty is not
established: progressive conversion is prior art in
[Network of Theseus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04198), and aligned internal
intervention distillation is prior art in
[Causal Distillation](https://aclanthology.org/2022.naacl-main.318/).
